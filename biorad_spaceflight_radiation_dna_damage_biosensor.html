Quantum Coherent $\pi$-Stack Transport & Bethe-Bloch Bragg Cleavage Physics

The BioRad biosensor bridges cellular biology and solid-state quantum transport. Synthetic double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) oligomers immobilized across gold nanoelectrodes serve as active ballistic conductor channels. 1. Ballistic Charge Transport via Molecular $\pi$-Stack

In intact synthetic dsDNA, overlapping aromatic base pairs form a continuous $\pi$-conjugated corridor. Electrical conductance $G$ follows the Landauer-Büttiker formulation for 1D quasi-ballistic molecular wires, modulated by base-pair coupling transmission probabilities $T(E_F)$:

$$G = \frac{2e^2}{h} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{strands}}} \mathcal{T}_i(E_F) \cdot \exp\left(-\beta_{\text{hop}} L_i\right)$$

Where $e^2/h \approx 38.74\ \mu\text{S}$ is the quantum conductance limit, $\mathcal{T}_i$ is the coherent electron tunneling transmission probability, $\beta_{\text{hop}} \approx 0.04\ \text{Å}^{-1}$ is the low-attenuation hopping constant in pristine $\pi$-stacks, and $L_i$ is the inter-electrode span (nominal 34 nm = 100 base pairs).

2. Bethe-Bloch Ion Cleavage & Linear Energy Transfer

Galactic cosmic rays are dominated by relativistic heavy ions (HZE particles like $^{56}\text{Fe}^{26+}$) possessing colossal Linear Energy Transfer (LET) as quantified by the relativistic Bethe-Bloch stopping power equation:

$$-\frac{dE}{dx} = 2\pi N_A r_e^2 m_e c^2 \rho \frac{Z}{A} \frac{z_{\text{eff}}^2}{\beta^2} \left[ \ln\left(\frac{2m_e c^2 \beta^2 \gamma^2 W_{\max}}{I^2}\right) - 2\beta^2 \right]$$

Because stopping power scales with $z_{\text{eff}}^2$, an iron ion ($z=26$) deposits over **676 times more energy per micron** than a solar wind proton ($z=1$). This dense cylindrical plasma core (diameter $< 2.5\ \text{nm}$) instantly produces double-strand breaks (DSBs), terminating the $\pi$-stack channel permanently ($\mathcal{T}_i \to 0$).

3. State Observer Inversion: Discriminating $^1\text{H}^+$ vs $^{56}\text{Fe}$

Protons create mild electronic excitation and transient single-strand breaks (SSBs) that spontaneously anneal. Conversely, high-energy heavy ions destroy both sugar-phosphate backbones simultaneously. The onboard Kalman State Observer solves:

$$\mathbf{X}_{k+1} = \mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{X}_k + \mathbf{\Gamma} \begin{bmatrix} \dot{E}_{\text{dep}} \\ \Delta G(t) \end{bmatrix} + \mathbf{w}_k, \quad \mathcal{H}_{\text{hazard}} = \frac{|\Delta G / \Delta t|}{\int E \, dE} > \Theta_{\text{critical}}$$

When the ratio of instantaneous conductance loss step-function $|\Delta G/\Delta t|$ to the energy deposition rate breaches threshold $\Theta_{\text{critical}} = 0.42\ \text{nS}/\text{MeV}$, the event is definitively classified as an HZE GCR core rather than a solar proton ripple.
